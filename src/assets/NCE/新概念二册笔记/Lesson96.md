##### Lesson 96 The dead return
First listen and then answer the question.
What happens to the lanterns at the end of the festival?
A Festival for the Dead is held once a year in Japan.
This festival is a cheerful occasion, for on this day, the dead are said to return to their homes and they are welcomed by the living.
As they are expected to be hungry after their long journey, food is laid out for them.
Specially-made lanterns are hung outside each house to help the dead to find their way.
All night long, people dance and sing.
In the early morning, the food that had been laid out for the dead is thrown into a river or into the sea
as it is considered unlucky for anyone living to eat it.
In towns that are near the sea, the tiny lanterns which had been hung in the streets the night before, are placed into the water when the festival is over.
Thousands of lanterns slowly drift out to sea guiding the dead on their return journey to the other world.
This is a moving spectacle, for crowds of people stand on the shore watching the lanterns drifting away until they can be seen no more.

Japan holds a festival for the dead once a year and that day is a cheerful occasion. On that day, the dead will return to their home and will be welcomed by the living. Food is laid out for them after their long journey and lanterns made are lit to guide the dead on their way home.
In the evening, people all dance and sing until early morning. After their dancing and singing, they will throw away this uneaten food which is considered unlucky for anyone living to eat. In some towns near the sea, they will throw the tiny lanterns into the sea and they will stand on the shore and see them drifting away until they can be seen no more.
